Part 1: The 6 Options Explained Simply

When you execute the master test runner, you can choose from 6 testing modes tailored for different verification stages:

./testing/PathPlanner/run_testing_suite.sh
# Option Name Execution Mode What It Does When to Use It
1 Live Interactive Test Head (RViz2) Launches erc_path_planner, mock kinematics (50 Hz), mock perception, and opens RViz2 for a selected map. When you want to watch the rover drive live, see it swerve around rocks in real time, or click 2D Goal Pose manually.
2 Headless Batch Benchmark Headless (CLI) Runs all 9 Mars Yard maps sequentially in the background at maximum speed with zero graphics overhead. When you want a fast, automated Pass/Fail report across every terrain scenario with HTML & PDF dashboards.
3 Visual Batch Benchmark Head (RViz2) Runs all 9 benchmark maps automatically in sequence while keeping RViz2 open so you can observe transitions visually. When you want to visually verify that the rover clears canyons, labyrinths, and craters across all scenarios without getting trapped.
4 Single Scenario Benchmark Configurable Runs and scores just one specific map you choose (e.g. canyon_gate_passage), in head or headless mode. When tuning a specific controller parameter (turning radius, MPPI rollout weight) on one difficult obstacle layout.
5 Verify Maps & Paths Instant Generates 2D reference PNG map plots and reference waypoints into the reports/ folder. Sanity check to confirm all 9 scenario maps, start/goal poses, and intermediate waypoints are drawn accurately.
6 External Module Test Universal Starts physics simulation, mock perception, and RViz, but does not launch any planner. It listens for your external node. When you write a new custom algorithm or motor driver in another terminal and want the tester to evaluate it.

Part 2: How to Test the Main Model Step-by-Step

Choose one of the following methods depending on whether you want visual inspection or automated numerical scoring:

Method 1: Visual Live Test

Interactive Head Mode

  1. Run ./testing/PathPlanner/run_testing_suite.sh
  2. Type 1 and press Enter.
  3. Select a scenario (e.g. press Enter for scattered_rocks_detour).
  4. Press Enter to accept defaults (Y).
  5. RViz2 pops up: Watch the green path, cyan MPPI rollouts, and the 3D rover driving through terrain.

Method 2: Headless Scorecard

Fast Headless CI/CD

  1. Run the batch benchmark shortcut:
    ./testing/PathPlanner/run_testing_suite.sh --headless
  2. Wait ~45 seconds as all 9 scenarios are evaluated.
  3. Open the generated HTML dashboard in your browser:
    google-chrome testing/PathPlanner/reports/numerical_report.html

Method 3: External Custom Node

Black-Box Evaluation

  1. Start the universal test harness:
    ./testing/PathPlanner/run_testing_suite.sh --external
  2. In your own terminal, launch your custom planner node.
  3. Your node listens to /map and publishes to /cmd_vel. The tester tracks and scores all metrics!

Part 3: Head Mode vs. Headless Mode

The tester natively supports both Head Mode (GUI Visualization) and Headless Mode (Background Automation) to accommodate both developer inspection and CI/CD pipelines.

GUI / RViz2 Active

🖥️ Head Mode

Interactive visualization with live 3D rendering of the rover and sensor clouds.

  • Live 3D Rover Body: Watch base_link steer and follow Reeds-Shepp curves.
  • MPPI Trajectory Fan: Inspect the cyan cloud of 2,000 candidate rollouts adapting at 20 Hz.
  • Dynamic 3D Obstacles: Pop-up rocks render as red/orange 3D markers in RViz.
  • Interactive Goal Setting: Click 2D Goal Pose anytime to test arbitrary waypoints.
Launch Commands:
./testing/PathPlanner/run_testing_suite.sh (Select 1 or 3)
ros2 launch global_path_benchmarking live_test.launch.py use_rviz:=true
Zero GUI / Fast CI

⚡ Headless Mode

Pure background execution designed for fast batch testing and continuous integration.

  • Maximum Speed: Evaluates all 9 Mars Yard scenarios in ~45 seconds.
  • Zero Graphics Overhead: Runs on headless servers, Docker, and GitHub Actions without X11.
  • Complete Data Capture: Records all 13 planning and control telemetry metrics.
  • Automated Export: Compiles HTML dashboards, PDF reports, and CSV tables upon completion.
Launch Commands:
./testing/PathPlanner/run_testing_suite.sh --headless
ros2 launch global_path_benchmarking benchmark.launch.py use_rviz:=false

Part 4: Dynamic Obstacle Injection & Avoidance

Yes, the tester can insert dynamic obstacles during active navigation! It supports both automated pre-scheduled hazard pop-ups and live interactive obstacle injection to test how the rover reacts and swerves mid-run.

1. How to Insert Obstacles

Two Injection Methods

A. Scheduled via Scenario YAML:
In config/scenarios.yaml, specify dynamic obstacles with a trigger time (seconds into the run):

dynamic_obstacles:
  - trigger_time: 0.5
    x: 1.0, y: 2.5, radius: 0.4

B. Live On-the-Fly Injection:
Spawn an obstacle mid-motion via ROS 2 topic:

ros2 topic pub --once /mock_perception/spawn_obstacle geometry_msgs/msg/Point "{x: 1.5, y: 2.0, z: 0.2}"

2. How the Rover Avoids Them

Two-Tier Safety Architecture

Tier 1: Local MPPI Controller Swerving:
The local costmap (10m × 10m rolling window) updates at 20 Hz. MPPI evaluates 2,000 trajectory rollouts. Its ObstaclesCritic heavily penalizes candidate paths touching the obstacle, steering the rover smoothly around the rock without stopping.

Tier 2: Global Smac Hybrid A* Detour:
If the obstacle completely blocks a narrow canyon corridor (e.g. canyon_gate_blocked), the BT navigator triggers Smac Hybrid A* to calculate a brand new global detour highway using Reeds-Shepp kinematics.

3. How You Know It Avoided Them

Visual & Quantitative Telemetry
  • Live in RViz2: Watch the cyan MPPI cloud flex and bend around the newly appeared red rock marker.
  • Trajectory Map (PNG): The output plot draws the original path vs. the Cyan Detour Path around the orange obstacle.
  • Replanning Score: Verified at 100 / 100 with detour time recorded.
  • Safety Clearance: Proves the rover kept ≥ 0.35m margin with 0 collisions.

Preventing Overwrites: Isolated Historical Archives

To maintain an unblemished historical record of your regression testing, benchmark runs are organized into isolated, timestamped session folders:

testing/PathPlanner/reports/ ├── run_20260907_143000/ # Baseline benchmark session │ ├── result_scenario_canyon_gate.png │ ├── numerical_report.html │ ├── numerical_report.pdf │ └── results.csv ├── run_20260907_161500/ # Controller tuning session │ ├── result_scenario_canyon_gate.png │ ├── numerical_report.html │ ├── numerical_report.pdf │ └── results.csv └── latest/ # Symlink pointing to most recent run

Key Tip: In the launcher menu, when prompted: Clean stale test reports before running? [Y/n], type n to preserve previous session folders, or pass clean:=false in your launch commands.

Part 6: How to Know Your Model "Works Well"

You know your autonomous navigation and motion control stack is working properly when you observe these 4 Green Lights:

Criterion 1

The Pass Verdict

At the end of each test, check the summary box in the terminal:

PLANNER SCORE: 92.5 / 100
CONTROLLER SCORE: 90.1 / 100
SYSTEM SCORE: 91.3 / 100
OUTCOME: PASS (≥ 85)

A score ≥ 85.0 confirms the system is competition-ready.

Criterion 2

Zero Collisions

Verify the collision count:

Blocked Cells: 0
Safety Margin: ≥ 0.35 m

Blocked Cells must be 0. This proves the rover never grazed a rock, crater wall, or canyon cliff.

Criterion 3

Smooth Driving & Energy

Look at the 3 key motion control indicators:

Mean CTE: ≤ 0.10 m
Chatter: ≤ 5 reversals
Control Effort: low Ju

Means the rover drove tightly along the route without oscillating, hunting, or draining excessive battery.

Criterion 4

Visual Confirmation

What to verify in RViz2:

  • Green Path (/plan): Curves smoothly (min radius ≥ 0.8m) rather than making sharp right angles.
  • Cyan Cloud (/local_plan): MPPI trajectories fan out and swerve around newly detected pop-up rocks.
  • 3D Rover: Reaches destination pins cleanly without spinning or stalling.
